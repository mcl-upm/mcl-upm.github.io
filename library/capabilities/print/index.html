Welcome

Begin with the question your material must answer

MCL provides material characterisation and analytical testing within KKA at UPM's Faculty of Engineering. Its stated mission is to deliver reliable, high-quality services to academic and industrial partners, supported by expertise in analytical testing, metallurgy, polymers and advanced materials.

For a useful first enquiry, describe what the material is and what needs to be established. A question about melting behaviour leads to a different method from a question about elemental concentration or particle size. The published catalogue groups services by these measurement needs.

Send the sample description, requested analysis and reporting requirements to the quotation contacts. The current application form records applicant, payment and sample details; completion dates are agreed for the individual request.

Connect the method to the material

Microscopy describes visible surface features at high magnification. Surface-area and particle-size measurements answer different questions: how much surface is accessible and how particle sizes are distributed. Elemental methods measure elemental content, while viscometry examines resistance to flow.

Combined services require careful selection. FESEM-EDX adds elemental information to electron microscopy. The FESEM-Raman option connects imaging with molecular information. The listed price for one configuration must not be treated as the price for a different detector, preparation or combined measurement.

Water analysis and other specialist options on the request form are introduced on page 14. They are not additional proof of equipment housed within MCL. Discuss the relevant laboratory, method, preparation and quotation before booking.

Thermal

Follow a material through temperature

Effective July 2025 · final price by quotation

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) · RM230. DSC records heat absorbed or released during a controlled temperature programme. MCL lists glass transition, melting, crystallisation, heat capacity and oxidation behaviour among its thermal-analysis applications.

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) · RM230. TGA records changes in sample mass as temperature changes. The listed applications include pyrolysis profiles and compositional analysis, helping users investigate thermal breakdown and material stability.

DSC and TGA provide complementary information: a thermal transition can occur without a corresponding mass change. Discuss which behaviour needs to be measured and the intended temperature programme when requesting analysis. Both services are outside the accredited scope.

Chromatography · GC

Separate a complex mixture

Effective July 2025 · final price by quotation

Gas chromatography with flame-ionisation detection (GC-FID) · RM130*. GC separates components before a flame detector records organic compounds. MCL lists an Agilent GC-FID service.

Gas chromatography with thermal-conductivity detection (GC-TCD) · RM80*. This configuration detects separated components through changes in thermal conductivity. MCL lists an Agilent GC-TCD service.

Gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) · RM380*. GC-MS separates volatile components and uses mass patterns to support identification. MCL lists a Shimadzu GC-MS service.

Chromatography · HPLC

Choose detection for a liquid separation

Effective July 2025 · final price by quotation

High-performance liquid chromatography with UV detection (HPLC-UV) · RM105*. HPLC separates components carried in a liquid mobile phase. UV detection measures separated compounds that absorb ultraviolet light; the published service uses a Shimadzu system.

HPLC with refractive-index or evaporative light-scattering detection (RI/ELSD) · RM100*. These detector options record refractive-index changes or light scattered by particles formed after evaporation. MCL lists an Agilent HPLC RI/ELSD service.

HPLC with refractive-index/photodiode-array detection (RI/PDA) · RM100*. RI and PDA offer different information from a liquid separation, including refractive-index response and UV/visible spectra. The published service uses a Jasco system.

All configurations are outside the accredited scope. Effective July 2025 · final price by quotation. MCL to explain the HPLC price asterisk and which detector options the price covers.

Surface & particle

Measure the spaces between particles

Brunauer–Emmett–Teller surface-area analysis (BET) · RM425. Gas adsorption is used to investigate accessible surface area and pore structure. MCL lists a Micromeritics analyser; the application form also offers Langmuir analysis.

Particle-size analysis · RM55. The service measures the distribution of particle sizes rather than a single visual dimension. The published catalogue lists a Malvern analyser and a stated range of 0.1–1000 µm.

Surface area and particle size describe different aspects of a material. A particle-size distribution alone does not establish its pore structure, and surface area does not replace a size distribution. Discuss sample form and the information required before selecting one or both services.

Both services are outside the accredited scope. Effective July 2025 · final price by quotation.

Elemental

Ask which elements are present

CHNS elemental analysis · CHN RM260; S RM250. The LECO analyser measures carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen and sulphur content. The published brochure lists CHN and sulphur as separate price entries.

Flame atomic absorption spectrophotometry (AAS) · RM80** per element. AAS measures metal concentrations in liquid samples through element-specific light absorption. MCL lists a Thermo Scientific flame AAS service.

Inductively coupled plasma–optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES) · RM190. ICP-OES measures elemental concentrations from light emitted by a plasma. The published service uses a Perkin Elmer instrument.

These services are outside the accredited scope. Effective July 2025 · final price by quotation. MCL to explain the AAS double asterisk and ICP-OES quotation basis.

How to send a sample

Prepare a sample that can be accepted

1. Discuss the request. Email the sample description, intended analysis and reporting needs. MCL's named quotation contacts are Dr. Dayang Radiah Awang Biak and ChM. Shafizah Masuri.

2. Complete the current form. Use F3-MCL, effective 18 June 2026, issue 1, revision 3. Record the applicant, payment details, samples and requested services.

3. Identify every sample. Use the form's sample-ID sheet and keep each sample identifier consistent across the containers and request. Confirm preparation, quantity and delivery arrangements with MCL. MCL to confirm technique-specific quantities, containers and acceptance instructions.

4. Agree payment arrangements. Published routes include the UPM payment gateway, LO/PO and UPM internal vot transfer. Follow the confirmed quotation and payment instructions.

5. Agree the output and completion date. Select raw data or a test report as applicable; an accredited test requires a report. Completion is agreed individually.

Sustainability

Good science includes responsible practice

Environmental care, people and accountable practice all matter in an analytical laboratory. Responsible operation includes careful handling of chemicals, appropriate waste segregation and sensible use of energy. These principles explain the E, S and G labels shown here; they are not measured claims about MCL's environmental performance.

The public record includes a KKA STEM outreach programme at SMK Convent Kajang, documented in an official gallery published on 6 December 2024. The event date is not stated. This is departmental outreach evidence, rather than proof that every pictured participant represents MCL.

MCL's published workshop announcements provide a further route into analytical learning. Its public brochure makes service prices available, while the current SAMM 1265 schedule defines the accredited polymer FTIR-ATR test separately from the wider catalogue.

MCL to confirm implemented environmental practices and provide approved evidence before any quantified environmental claim is added.
