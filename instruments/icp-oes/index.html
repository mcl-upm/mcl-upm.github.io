What it tells you

Check several elemental concentrations from one prepared sample. ICP-OES measures the characteristic light emitted by excited atoms and ions in an argon plasma. It helps you investigate contamination, compare raw materials and assess batch consistency. Calibration converts emission intensity to concentration; digestion and dilution establish the link to your original material.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1How much of each requested element is present?
  2. Q2Has my process introduced metal contamination?
  3. Q3Do my raw-material batches have consistent elemental profiles?
  4. Q4How do my treated and untreated samples compare?

How it works

Inside the ICP-OES, step by step

  1. A nebuliser turns your prepared liquid into a fine aerosol.
  2. Argon carries it into a plasma sustained by a radiofrequency field. Heat dries the droplets, breaks compounds apart and excites atoms and ions.
  3. As these species release energy, they emit light at characteristic wavelengths. Optics separate that light for measurement.
  4. Calibration converts emission intensity into concentration for each selected element. Digestion and dilution factors connect those values to your original sample. Spectral overlap and matrix effects require suitable wavelength selection and correction.
