What it tells you

Find out how much of a selected metal your sample contains. Flame AAS measures elemental concentrations in prepared liquids, helping you check contamination, compare batches or assess raw materials. Calibration links light absorption to concentration. The result describes the element's amount, rather than its chemical form.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1How much of my target metal is present?
  2. Q2Does my sample meet my metal-content specification?
  3. Q3Do my batches contain similar metal levels?
  4. Q4Has processing introduced metal contamination?

How it works

Inside the AAS, step by step

  1. A nebuliser turns your prepared liquid into a fine mist.
  2. The mist enters a flame. Droplets dry, and compounds break apart into free atoms.
  3. A lamp sends light specific to your target element through the flame. Those atoms absorb some of it.
  4. A detector measures the lost light. Calibration standards convert absorbance into concentration, with dilution and digestion factors linking it back to your original sample. Each element needs its own measurement settings.
