What it tells you

Connect the features in an electron image with the vibrational signatures of your material. Integrated Raman imaging helps you compare carbon forms, minerals and other phases at selected locations. Electron microscopy supplies surface detail; Raman spectra add information about bonding and structure. Together they help explain why neighbouring regions behave differently.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1Do these surface features have different Raman signatures?
  2. Q2Where is my selected material phase located?
  3. Q3How do carbon regions differ across my sample?
  4. Q4Does a visible defect coincide with a chemical change?

How it works

Inside the FESEM-RISE, step by step

  1. A focused electron beam scans your specimen to build a detailed surface image.
  2. The integrated Raman system focuses a laser at selected positions. MCL lists a 532 nm laser.
  3. A small fraction of the scattered light changes energy through molecular or lattice vibrations. A spectrometer resolves these shifts into Raman bands.
  4. Repeating the measurement across a grid creates a band map for comparison with the electron image. Fluorescence, focus and laser heating affect the signal; each image samples a different interaction volume.
