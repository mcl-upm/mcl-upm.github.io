What it tells you

Measure known UV-absorbing compounds within a dissolved mixture. HPLC separates components before a UV detector measures their absorbance, helping you compare formulations, check ingredient levels and investigate process changes. Suitable standards connect peak area to concentration. Detector wavelength determines which compounds produce a useful signal.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1How much of my target ingredient is present?
  2. Q2Do my batches have matching peak patterns?
  3. Q3Has storage changed my UV-absorbing components?
  4. Q4Does my target separate from other sample components?

How it works

Inside the HPLC-UV, step by step

  1. A pump drives liquid mobile phase through a packed column under pressure.
  2. Your injected sample travels through the packing. Different interactions separate its compounds into bands.
  3. Each band passes a UV flow cell. Compounds that absorb at the selected wavelength reduce light reaching the detector.
  4. The signal becomes a peak over time. Comparing retention with standards supports assignment; calibrated peak areas give concentrations. Compounds with little absorption at that wavelength give weak signals, so wavelength selection is part of method design.
