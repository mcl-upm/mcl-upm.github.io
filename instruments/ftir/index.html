What it tells you

Find the molecular signatures that help identify your material and explain differences between samples. FTIR measures infrared absorption by vibrating chemical bonds. Use it to compare polymers, investigate contamination or check changes after processing. Attenuated total reflection (ATR) examines material touching a crystal; KBr transmission measures a prepared pellet. Reference spectra help connect absorption bands with identity.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1Which polymer does my sample match?
  2. Q2What functional groups are present in my material?
  3. Q3Has processing changed my material’s infrared spectrum?
  4. Q4Does this contaminant differ from my original material?

How it works

Inside the FTIR, step by step

  1. Infrared light enters an interferometer, where a beam splitter sends it towards fixed and moving mirrors.
  2. The returning beams recombine. Their changing path difference encodes the light's wavelengths in an interferogram.
  3. Light interacts with your sample at an ATR crystal or passes through a KBr pellet. Molecular vibrations absorb characteristic frequencies.
  4. A detector records the signal. A Fourier transform converts it into a spectrum, revealing absorption bands for comparison with references. Good contact, dry preparation and a suitable background keep those comparisons meaningful.
