What it tells you

See how the components of your gas mixture separate and compare their calibrated amounts. GC-TCD detects changes in thermal conductivity as each component leaves a column. It supports gas-composition checks and process comparisons. Sensitivity depends on the difference between each analyte and the carrier gas, so method choice matters.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1What proportion of my target gas is present?
  2. Q2Do my process-gas batches have consistent composition?
  3. Q3Has my gas mixture changed after treatment?
  4. Q4Which components separate under the chosen method?

How it works

Inside the GC-TCD, step by step

  1. A measured portion of your sample enters the column with carrier gas.
  2. Components interact differently with the stationary phase and emerge at different times.
  3. The effluent passes a heated sensing element. Each component changes how readily the gas carries heat away, altering the element's temperature or resistance relative to a reference stream.
  4. The circuit turns these changes into chromatographic peaks. Standards relate their areas to concentration. Carrier-gas choice, stable flow and detector temperature control the response.
