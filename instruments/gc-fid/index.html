What it tells you

Compare volatile organic components and quantify known targets with suitable standards. GC-FID separates a mixture into peaks and measures the response as compounds burn in a flame. It helps you check batch consistency, solvent content and product composition. Calibration links peak area to amount; retention comparisons support target assignments.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1How much of my target organic compound is present?
  2. Q2Do my batches have consistent peak patterns?
  3. Q3Has my process changed the volatile composition?
  4. Q4Are unexpected organic peaks present in my sample?

How it works

Inside the GC-FID, step by step

  1. Your prepared sample enters a heated inlet and vaporises.
  2. Carrier gas moves it through a coated column. Compounds travel at different speeds because their volatility and interactions with the coating differ.
  3. Separated compounds enter a hydrogen-air flame. Many organic compounds generate ions, producing a measurable current.
  4. The detector draws a peak as each band passes. Comparing peak areas with calibration standards gives target amounts. Retention time alone does not establish identity, and response varies between compounds.
