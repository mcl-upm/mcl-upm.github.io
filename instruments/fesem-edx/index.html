What it tells you

Look closely at cracks, particles, coatings and fracture surfaces. FESEM builds detailed electron images to help you investigate defects and compare material structure. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) adds elemental evidence from selected regions, connecting what you see with which elements are present. Its elemental estimates depend on specimen geometry, coating and measurement conditions.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1What does my fracture surface reveal?
  2. Q2Are my particles uniform in shape?
  3. Q3Which elements are present in this defect?
  4. Q4Where are different elements distributed across my sample?

How it works

Inside the FESEM-EDX, step by step

  1. Your mounted specimen enters a vacuum chamber.
  2. A field-emission source sends a focused electron beam across it, line by line. Electron signals build an image at each beam position.
  3. Secondary electrons highlight surface form; backscattered electrons often distinguish regions with different average atomic numbers.
  4. EDX sorts emitted X-rays by energy to show elemental signals. Repeating this across an area builds a map. X-rays come from a volume beneath the surface, so coating, beam conditions and specimen geometry matter when interpreting elemental estimates.
