What it tells you

Understand the properties that matter to your water-treatment or process decision. BOD, COD and TOC describe oxygen demand and organic carbon; turbidity, solids and pH describe other distinct characteristics. Water tests are run by our sister laboratory, the Environmental Engineering Laboratory. Use the selected parameters to compare samples and investigate treatment changes.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1How has treatment changed my water’s oxygen demand?
  2. Q2How much organic carbon is in my sample?
  3. Q3How cloudy is my water, and how much solid remains?
  4. Q4What is my sample’s pH?

How it works

Water tests, step by step

  1. Separate portions of your water sample follow the selected test methods.
  2. BOD (biochemical oxygen demand) compares dissolved oxygen before and after defined incubation; COD (chemical oxygen demand) measures oxidant consumption during digestion. TOC (total organic carbon) converts organic carbon to carbon dioxide for measurement.
  3. Turbidity measures light scattering. Suspended solids are collected on a filter and weighed after drying; dissolved solids are measured from dried residue of a filtered portion.
  4. A calibrated electrode measures pH. Each result describes a different property, so interpretation uses its method, units and sampling conditions.
