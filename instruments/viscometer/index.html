What it tells you

Measure how strongly your liquid or paste resists a rotating spindle. Rotational viscometry helps you compare batches, develop formulations and investigate handling or application problems. Temperature, spindle and speed define the result. For fluids whose viscosity changes with shear, comparing readings under matched conditions is essential.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1Does my batch meet its viscosity specification?
  2. Q2Has reformulation made my product thicker or thinner?
  3. Q3Does my sample’s apparent viscosity change with speed?
  4. Q4How does temperature affect my product’s flow resistance?

How it works

Inside the VISCOMETER, step by step

  1. A selected spindle or geometry is immersed in your sample.
  2. The instrument rotates it at a controlled speed. Viscous drag creates resistance, measured as torque.
  3. Geometry and speed convert torque into viscosity. Temperature is controlled because warming or cooling can change flow resistance.
  4. Readings at selected speeds or temperatures help compare behaviour. For non-Newtonian fluids, apparent viscosity varies with shear conditions. Mixing history, bubbles and settling also matter, so comparisons use the same preparation and measurement conditions.
