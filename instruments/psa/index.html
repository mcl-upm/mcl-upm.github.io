What it tells you

Compare the size distribution of your dispersed particles. Laser diffraction helps you check milling, formulation and batch consistency by converting a scattering pattern into equivalent-sphere diameters. Percentiles such as D10, D50 and D90 describe the distribution. Dispersion conditions determine whether you measure individual particles, agglomerates or a mixture of both.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1What is my sample’s median particle size?
  2. Q2How broad is my particle-size distribution?
  3. Q3Has milling reduced my coarse-particle fraction?
  4. Q4Do my powder batches have consistent size distributions?

How it works

Inside the PSA, step by step

  1. Your sample is dispersed so particles pass through a laser beam.
  2. Detectors collect the angular pattern of scattered light. Larger particles mainly scatter at smaller angles; finer particles contribute more at wider angles.
  3. Software fits an optical model to the pattern, using suitable refractive-index assumptions to estimate equivalent-sphere diameters.
  4. The result becomes a size distribution with selected percentiles. Dispersion energy, concentration and bubbles affect the measurement. D50 marks the midpoint on the stated distribution basis; it describes neither particle shape nor every particle individually.
