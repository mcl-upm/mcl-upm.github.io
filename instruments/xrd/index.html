What it tells you

Find which crystalline phases are consistent with your material's diffraction pattern. Powder XRD helps you investigate raw materials, compare processing changes and assess phase composition. Crystal structures produce characteristic groups of peaks for comparison with references. Amorphous material and low-abundance phases may need complementary analysis.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1Which crystalline phases are present in my material?
  2. Q2Has heating changed my crystal phases?
  3. Q3Do my batches have consistent diffraction patterns?
  4. Q4Are the expected crystalline peaks present in my sample?

How it works

Inside the XRD, step by step

  1. X-rays strike a prepared specimen containing many crystallites in different orientations.
  2. The instrument scans angle while a detector measures scattered intensity.
  3. Where crystal planes satisfy Bragg's condition, scattered waves reinforce and form diffraction peaks.
  4. The pattern builds as intensity against 2θ. Comparing groups of peaks with reference patterns supports phase identification. Preferred orientation changes intensities; small crystallites, strain and instrument effects broaden peaks. Quantitative phase analysis or crystallite-size estimates need appropriate models and corrections.
