What it tells you

See when your material loses mass as it heats and how much remains. TGA helps you compare thermal stability, investigate volatile content and assess residue in feedstocks or formulations. Distinct mass-loss steps reveal stages of change. Their chemical assignment depends on sample composition and atmosphere; the balance measures mass rather than identifying released gases.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1At what temperature does my material start losing mass?
  2. Q2How much residue remains after my heating programme?
  3. Q3Do my batches decompose in similar stages?
  4. Q4Does my material behave differently in an oxidising atmosphere?

How it works

Inside the TGA, step by step

  1. Your sample sits in a pan on a sensitive balance inside a furnace.
  2. The furnace follows a temperature programme while purge gas sets the surrounding atmosphere.
  3. The balance continuously tracks mass. Moisture release, evaporation and decomposition can create distinct downward steps.
  4. A curve plots remaining mass against temperature or time. Its derivative highlights where mass changes fastest. Final residue helps compare formulations, while heating rate, atmosphere and sample preparation set the conditions for a fair comparison. Identifying released compounds needs a complementary technique.
