What it tells you

Extend liquid chromatography to compounds that absorb little UV light. Refractive-index (RI) detection measures changes in refractive index; evaporative light-scattering detection (ELSD) measures light scattered by particles left after solvent evaporation. These complementary responses help you compare sugars, lipids and other suitable dissolved components. Detector choice, mobile phase and calibration determine what you can measure.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1Can I measure ingredients with weak UV absorption?
  2. Q2How much of my target compound is present?
  3. Q3Do my sugar or lipid batches compare consistently?
  4. Q4Which detector suits my analyte and solvent?

How it works

Inside the HPLC-RI-ELSD, step by step

  1. A pump carries your injected solution through a packed column, separating compounds by their interactions with the stationary phase.
  2. With RI detection, the effluent passes an optical cell that compares its refractive index with the mobile phase. Temperature and solvent composition must remain stable.
  3. With ELSD, the effluent becomes a mist. Solvent evaporates, leaving analyte particles that scatter a light beam.
  4. The detector records peaks as separated bands arrive. Calibration accounts for detector response; ELSD often needs a nonlinear calibration. These signals support comparison with standards rather than identification alone.
