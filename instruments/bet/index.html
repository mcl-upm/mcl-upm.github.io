What it tells you

Compare the gas-accessible surface area of your powders and porous solids. BET analysis helps you assess adsorbents, catalysts and processing changes where available surface matters. A gas-adsorption isotherm records uptake as pressure changes; fitting the appropriate region estimates surface area per unit mass. Pore volume and size require separate models.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1How much accessible surface does my material have?
  2. Q2Has activation increased my surface area?
  3. Q3How do my adsorbent batches compare?
  4. Q4What does gas uptake reveal about my pores?

How it works

Inside the BET, step by step

  1. A weighed sample is degassed to clear moisture and adsorbed contaminants from its surfaces.
  2. The sample tube is cooled under controlled conditions, commonly for nitrogen adsorption.
  3. Gas enters in measured doses. At each relative pressure, the analyser records how much your material takes up, building an adsorption isotherm.
  4. BET fitting estimates the gas needed for a single molecular layer, then converts that amount into surface area. Pore calculations use additional models. Preparation and fit selection define the meaning of the result.
