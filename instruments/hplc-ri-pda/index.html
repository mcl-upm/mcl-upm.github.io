What it tells you

Separate your dissolved mixture and compare its components using absorbance or refractive-index (RI) response. A photodiode-array (PDA) detector records absorbance across multiple wavelengths, adding spectral evidence to chromatographic peaks. RI offers another route for suitable compounds with weak UV absorption. Use these signals and appropriate standards to investigate formulations and quantify known ingredients.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1What absorbance spectrum belongs to my target peak?
  2. Q2How much of my known ingredient is present?
  3. Q3Do my batches have matching chromatographic profiles?
  4. Q4Can refractive-index detection reveal my weak UV absorber?

How it works

Inside the HPLC-RI-PDA, step by step

  1. A pump pushes mobile phase and your injected sample through a packed column.
  2. Compounds interact differently with the packing and liquid, separating into bands.
  3. A PDA detector measures absorbance across wavelengths as each band crosses its flow cell. An RI detector instead measures refractive-index differences from the mobile phase.
  4. The signals form chromatograms; PDA adds spectra for selected peaks. Standards help assess identity and concentration. RI needs stable temperature and solvent composition, while PDA requires absorption within its measurement range. Retention time or spectral similarity alone cannot prove identity.
