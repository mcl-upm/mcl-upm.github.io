What it tells you

Investigate what is in your volatile mixture. GC-MS separates its components, then records the ion patterns associated with each peak. Comparing those patterns with reference spectra helps you investigate contaminants, raw materials and process changes. Library matches suggest identities; an authentic standard and retention evidence strengthen confirmation.

Questions it answers

  1. Q1Which compounds could explain my sample’s peaks?
  2. Q2What mass spectrum belongs to this contaminant?
  3. Q3How does my sample compare with a reference?
  4. Q4Has processing introduced unexpected volatile components?

How it works

Inside the GC-MS, step by step

  1. Your prepared sample vaporises in the inlet and enters a GC column with carrier gas.
  2. Compounds separate as they interact differently with the column coating.
  3. Each emerging band enters the mass spectrometer. Ionisation produces charged molecules and fragments, which the analyser separates by mass-to-charge ratio.
  4. A detector records ion abundance, building a chromatogram and spectra over time. Library comparison suggests candidate identities. Co-eluting compounds can mix their signals, so standards and retention information help test an assignment. Quantification requires calibration.
